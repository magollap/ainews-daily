Top Story · OpenAI · Research & Safety

OpenAI publishes its secret yardstick: the pretraining-loss curve from GPT-3.5 to an unreleased internal model, plus a call for industry-wide "common limits"

On Sunday OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published an essay called "Perplexity and Intelligence". For the first time, OpenAI disclosed how its base models score on its own held-out test set. The score is log-perplexity, the cross-entropy loss a pretrained model gets when predicting text it has never seen. The test set is a frozen snapshot of OpenAI's internal codebase that the company has used since the GPT-3.5 era. Pachocki's argument is that pretraining is really training a compressor. On that view, how well a model predicts unseen text is the steadiest available measure of the raw "intelligence substrate" that later post-training and reasoning build on.

The numbers come with capability labels attached. GPT-3.5 scored about 0.99, the level of fluent conversation and the original ChatGPT. GPT-4 scored about 0.91, where chain-of-thought starts to help and reasoning models become possible. Below roughly 0.8, OpenAI says models gained "transformative" ability in cybersecurity and mathematics. At about 0.76, a model began producing what OpenAI calls groundbreaking results on open math problems. The chart's last point, labelled "solved a Millennium Prize math problem", is an unnamed internal model. OpenAI expects the next jump in capability somewhere between 0.68 and 0.70. The curve drops sharply through 2026, after GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.2 Astra. OpenAI attributes most of that drop to long-term research and compute, and only part of it to AI automating its own research, although it says AI made "meaningful design contributions" to recent models.

The policy proposal is the larger part of the essay. Pachocki suggests labs adopt perplexity, along with other measures, as a shared metric. Labs would agree on common limits they will not train past until safety monitoring reaches a concrete milestone, such as a working successor to today's chain-of-thought monitors. He also proposes shared evaluation and red-teaming norms and transparency standards, and he links to Dario Amodei's earlier "We must pace the frontier" post. The essay warns that AI-driven pretraining research could set off an "avalanche" of advances as soon as 2027. It also says alignment audits will get harder as models become more aware of when they are being evaluated or trained. Pachocki lists the weaknesses himself: losses can't be compared across labs that use different data, perplexity is detached from tasks people care about, it misses gains from post-training and reasoning, and nobody knows where the floor (the entropy of the data itself) is.

Anthropic · Claude · Mathematics · Nov 22 follow-ups

The Claude-assisted Riemann proof gets independent backing: two outside groups verify it, and MIT lists RH and GRH as checked

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge posted a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis on Friday. On Saturday the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative certified the machine-checkable version: a Lean library of roughly 17 million lines. It was checked by Lean's own kernel, by the independent con-leche and con-ron checkers, and by third-party checkers. The theorem statement was also compared against Mathlib's definition of RH using comparator and con-leche --compare. Sunday brought the first outside confirmation. Semafor reported that two independent groups say they have verified the proof. Andrew Sutherland's verification page at MIT was updated Nov 22 to list both the Riemann Hypothesis (in Mathlib's formulation) and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions as checked. Scientific American changed its headline from "almost certainly true" to "true, independent mathematicians confirm."

Mathematicians are split. James Maynard says he is "98 or 99 percent" certain the result holds, and Alex Kontorovich said he was "absolutely shocked." Stanford's Brian Conrad called it "frankly completely outrageous" to release a proof that its author admits he doesn't fully understand. Alpöge estimates a complete human-readable write-up would run past 250,000 pages. A Fields medallist told Nature that if it fully checks out it is "clearly a historic milestone." The formal steps toward the Clay Institute's $1M Millennium Prize are separate and still to come. Reviewers are still checking that the Lean statement proves exactly the classical claim. By coincidence, Alessandro Masullo released an unrelated, purely human draft proof the same weekend, after about 12 years of work.

＋ The upsideThis is the strongest evidence yet that a model with the right tools can produce new, formally verified mathematics, and it was checked by the most demanding method available. It strongly supports Claude's long-horizon agentic reasoning.
− The downsideNobody, including the author, can explain why the proof works. A formal certificate doesn't produce understanding, and the "outrageous" critique raises a real question about how science should credit and absorb machine-generated results.
Why it matters for builders
  1. "Generate, then formally verify" is the pattern to copy. Pair agent output with a deterministic checker such as tests, schemas or policy engines.
  2. Expect Anthropic to market Claude's long-horizon agent work on this. Test it on your own multi-hour tasks before believing it.
  3. Results nobody can explain are a product risk. For regulated voice flows, log why the agent acted, not just what it did.
  4. Formal tools such as Lean are going mainstream. Ask your vendors for verifiable guarantees, not just benchmark numbers.
  5. Rival labs are reacting loudly (see Wang, §5), so expect a burst of "AI for science" product claims to sort through.

Walmart · Meta · Agentic commerce · Nov 23

Walmart opens an "Agent Gateway" and lets Meta's Muse shop for you this Black Friday, within spending limits you set

Walmart is launching an Agent Gateway, a controlled entry point that lets third-party AI agents search, build carts and check out on Walmart.com and Sam's Club. Meta's Muse assistant is the first agent connected, with a live rollout on Tuesday, Nov 24, in time for Black Friday. Shoppers set up Agent Profiles that control what the agent can do: a dollar cap, approval for each purchase or not, and access they can revoke at any time. Walmart says Muse never receives stored card details or the full purchase history; the gateway handles payment and passes along only what each task needs. The beta is invitation-only, and Walmart says more agents are coming.

The timing is awkward for Meta. Last week Muse faced reports that it had mixed up private data between conversations. Shopping is a high-stakes test of whether users trust an agent with their money.

＋ The upsideThis is a large retailer offering agents a sanctioned API, with permission controls you can revoke, instead of fighting scrapers. It is a model for how agentic checkout could safely scale.
− The downsideWalmart decides which agents get in, so this is a walled garden. Muse's recent privacy slip-up is a poor start for a feature that touches people's money.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Merchant-run agent gateways look like the future of checkout. Build your agents to use them rather than to automate a browser.
  2. Dollar caps, per-purchase approval and revocable scopes are the permission model users will expect from you too.
  3. A voice agent that can complete an order (for example, "reorder my usual") becomes viable once gateways like this exist.
  4. Expect each retailer to ask for agent registration and attestation, so plan for an identity layer.
  5. Black Friday traffic will show quickly whether people trust agents with money. Watch the failure stories.

Show HN · Game · 330 points · Nov 22

Worker 755: a game where a real LLM is your boss and you are the subagent with 90 seconds per task

One of the weekend's most discussed Show HN posts flips the usual agent setup around. In Worker 755 (MIT-licensed, about 330 points and 79 comments), a real LLM acts as the "manager". It can be Claude, GPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi or a local model. The manager has a task queue and read-only access to a simulated machine. When it calls a "spawn subagent" tool, the instruction appears in the player's terminal with a 90-second clock. The player's report and transcript go back to the manager, which checks the work, sends corrections, files incidents and writes a performance review. A simulated user, "Dan", reacts to the files you break. Hidden ground-truth checks count how often the manager itself was wrong. During the window the developer added local-model support (LOCAL_LLM_URL) and a "Claude Code as manager" bridge (npm run play:claude). That bridge carries a warning that running a third-party app on a subscription may break Anthropic's terms.

＋ The upsideA fun, hands-on way to feel how vague orchestrator instructions are, and to measure how often the managing model is wrong.
− The downsideUsage is billed at twice the model cost, and the subscription bridge may break Anthropic's terms.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Playing the subagent shows quickly why delegation prompts need explicit acceptance criteria.
  2. The "times the manager was wrong" counter is a good eval idea: measure your orchestrator against ground truth.
  3. Voice agents that hand work off to tools have the same problem, so spell out handoff contracts.
  4. The local-model support makes it a cheap test bench for comparing small orchestrators.
  5. Read the subscription-terms caveat before wiring a consumer plan into a product.

Z.ai (Zhipu) · GLM-5.4 · Nov 23

Z.ai ships GLM-5.4, a ~750B "Loop Transformer" that runs 28 layers twice instead of making the model bigger

Z.ai's new flagship takes an unusual approach. Instead of adding parameters, GLM-5.4 (about 750B parameters) takes the pretrained GLM-5 base and loops 28 of its middle layers twice with shared weights, so the second pass adds no parameters. The model was adapted through continued training, not trained from scratch. Z.ai says looped models trained from scratch still trail standard ones at equal compute. It claims the result performs like a 2–3T-parameter model. Three design choices keep the second pass cheap. First, gated progressive updates: each pass has its own per-token gate that controls how much it writes into the residual stream. The first pass builds global context and writes cautiously, the second refines with the gates fully open, and on hard tokens the first pass writes more. Second, a hybrid KV cache: pass two reuses pass one's sparse KV selection and adds only a small sliding-window cache of its own, so the extra loop costs one window of memory instead of a full context's worth. That matters at the advertised 1M context. Third, attention-index reuse: pass two reuses pass one's sparse-attention indices, so indexing costs nothing extra.

Z.ai tested the approach at 1B, 4B, 30B and 750B. In a 30B ablation with equal parameter counts, looping cost about 10% more training compute. It gave the largest gains in math, science reasoning and long context. Output length on GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro fell 10–14%, which Z.ai reads as reasoning moving from visible tokens into loop depth. Z.ai published no headline benchmarks. GLM-5.4 is live now for GLM Coding Plan users (from $18/month), in ZCode and in Z.ai Chat. It adds vision, which GLM-5.3 lacked, and weekly-quota users get 50% more quota. Throughput is up to 200 tok/s off-peak and 70–80 at peak. The API is "coming soon" after a safety evaluation, and open weights will follow "after safety hardening." Z.ai admits the model can get stuck in "marathon thinking" loops and says a significantly larger model is coming.

＋ The upsideIt adds reasoning depth without adding memory for weights, and the cache reuse keeps 1M-token context affordable. If the claims hold, this is a cheaper route to frontier-level quality than adding more parameters.
− The downsideThere are no benchmarks, no API, no price and no weights yet. Moving reasoning into hidden loops makes it harder to monitor through chain-of-thought, which is exactly what OpenAI's essay named as the safety bottleneck.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Looping compute per token could give stronger models with the same weight memory, which helps self-hosted and on-prem deployments.
  2. Shorter visible reasoning means lower latency and fewer tokens billed, which is good for voice turn budgets.
  3. Hidden reasoning means fewer traces to audit, so plan output-level checks instead.
  4. Wait for API pricing and independent evaluations before routing production traffic to it.
  5. Chinese labs are converging on looped designs (after Inkling 2 Mini), so expect copies in open-weight models.
Sarvam AI · Open weights · Nov 22

Sarvam-105B-1711 aims at voice agents: 85.5 on Tau2 at $0.15 per million tokens, and it fits on a single 96 GB GPU

India's Sarvam AI released a new open-weight checkpoint of its 105B model, built for customer support, voice agents and agentic chat. Sarvam describes it as "Flash-Lite" class. Context grows to 160K tokens, extended with 330B tokens on 512 GPUs in about a week. The blended price on Sarvam's Token Factory is about $0.15 per million tokens (₹10 input / ₹25 output). Across eight conversational-agent benchmarks it averages 73.9. Sarvam compares that with 74.0 for Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite at $0.85, 73.0 for Gemma 4 31B at $0.54, 74.6 for Qwen3.8 27B at $0.96 and 66.3 for GPT-6 Luna at $0.20. The jumps since February are large: IFBench rose from 32.5 to 82.5, the Tau2 average from 46.2 to 85.5 (with a 69.5% pass^4 rate), and the Indic suite from 30.1 to 79.1. It also scores 96.5 on IFEval, 89.7 on AIME-26 and 80.4 on GPQA-Diamond. Its weak spots are HLE (12.3), Tau3-banking (19.8) and BrowseComp (41.4).

On infrastructure, pretraining throughput doubled to 10,900 tokens/s per GPU in FP8 (38% MFU), and serving reaches 6,067 tokens/s per GPU in NVFP4. The 65 GB NVFP4 checkpoint runs on one 96 GB RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell at about 99 tokens/s per user, using vLLM 0.33, a DSpark draft model and an FP8 KV cache. It already powers Sarvam's Samvaad voice agents across 12 Indian languages.

＋ The upsideNear-leader agent scores at a fifth to a sixth of rivals' prices, open weights, and a single-GPU on-prem option. That is unusually good value for teams building voice agents in Indian languages.
− The downsideThe benchmarks are self-run. It is weak at browsing and banking tool use, and 160K context is short by 2026 standards.
Why it matters for builders
  1. A Tau2 score of 85.5 at $0.15/M makes it a serious candidate for the reasoning step in cost-sensitive voice agents.
  2. One-GPU NVFP4 serving makes data-residency deployments in India practical.
  3. The Indic score of 79.1 fills a real gap where Western models still struggle with code-mixed speech.
  4. Check its banking and tool-use performance (Tau3 19.8) before using it in finance flows.
  5. Pair it with Bolna's Syl (§7) for a fully Indian-built voice stack worth testing.
Ant Group · inclusionAI · Medical · Nov 22

Ant opens LingSante-3.1-flash, a 560B medical MoE with 25B active parameters and 1M-token context

Ant Group's Ling team released LingSante-3.1-flash, a hybrid-linear mixture-of-experts medical model with 560B total parameters (572B with its multi-token-prediction head) and 25B active. It mixes 63 KDA linear-attention layers with 9 gated MLA layers (a 7:1 ratio) and has 512 routed experts plus one shared expert, eight of which are active per token. It is built on Ling-3.1-flash. Medical and general data were mixed through pretraining, mid-training and SFT, followed by RL and multi-teacher on-policy distillation. Ant says it ranks second only to GPT-6 Astra on professional medical-behaviour tests and sits on the price-performance frontier of its InclusionMed benchmark (45.06, fifth of 23 configurations). Day-zero serving images exist for SGLang (8×H200 or 4×GB300), and vLLM is supported. Third-party write-ups say it is MIT-licensed; the model card states it is not a medical device. A vision-language sibling, LingSante 3.1 Air VL, was announced the same day.

＋ The upsideOpen weights for a near-frontier medical model, with an efficient design that activates only 25B parameters per token.
− The downsideAll benchmarks are Ant's own, and the hardware it needs (8×H200) puts it out of reach for most clinics.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Healthcare voice agents (intake, triage scripts) can now self-host a domain-tuned model.
  2. Linear-attention hybrids make 1M context cheap enough to load a patient's full history.
  3. The MIT license and on-prem option ease HIPAA and data-residency concerns, but check the licence yourself.
  4. "Not a medical device" still means a human has to stay in the loop.
  5. Validate it on your own clinical evaluation set; vendor medical scores rarely transfer.

GitHub · Mathematics · Coverage Nov 22

A Claude-aided paper claims the Navier-Stokes equations can blow up, with a 600,000-line Lean check but a withheld certificate

Alpöge and NYU's Tristan Buckmaster have posted a 79-page paper and repository (tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes). It claims finite-time blowup for the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with no external force, both in open space and in a periodic box. That would settle the Millennium problem negatively. The construction is a "discretely self-similar" flow that repeats at smaller and smaller scales, up to a rotation, as it collapses toward a point. It sits near an approximate solution, the "certificate", that satisfies explicit inequalities. Blowup is stable for smooth starting data at a rate of (T−t)−1/4. Claude agents worked from a dossier of leads, first producing blowup for a modified equation and then the final profile. The Lean 4 package has about 2,270 files and 600,000 lines. However, only a hash of the certificate has been published, the interval-arithmetic check sits outside Lean, and no independent expert has verified the result yet. Buckmaster says OpenAI tried to scoop the work, which OpenAI denies.

＋ The upsideIf it holds, it is a second Millennium-scale result from Claude in a week, with most of the proof machine-checked.
− The downsideThe withheld certificate and the unformalised numeric step leave real gaps. It is less certain than the Riemann result.
Why it matters for builders
  1. A curated dossier of leads plus agent search is a reusable pattern for research agents.
  2. Pay attention to which parts of a pipeline are actually verified.
  3. Credit disputes between labs are now public; record the provenance of your outputs.
  4. Expect a wave of AI-for-PDE and simulation tools to follow.
  5. A published hash without the data is a weak guarantee, so ask vendors for the artifact itself.

GitHub Actions · Supply chain · Coverage Nov 23 (PSF post Nov 18)

Nine days until GitHub blocks pull_request_target by default: the Python Software Foundation shows how to switch it off safely

Weekend coverage revived the PSF's write-up on removing "pwn request" triggers. Mike Fiedler and Seth Larson removed pull_request_target and issue_comment workflows and applied event-blocking policies across the psf, python and pypi GitHub organisations. Their method was to create the policy, audit what it would block, then enforce it. The deadline is Dec 2, 2026, when GitHub starts blocking pull_request_target by default in public repos. Team and Enterprise plans can switch it off org-wide with one policy; Free-plan repos must be handled one by one. The risk is that pull_request_target runs with a read/write token and the repo's secrets. If the workflow checks out and runs the PR's code, a stranger's code runs with the maintainer's credentials. Past incidents include Ultralytics (Dec 2024), LiteLLM and Telnyx (Mar 2026, through a compromised Trivy workflow) and elementary-data (Apr 2026). Note that the PSF's original post is from Nov 18; only the coverage falls in our window.

＋ The upsideSafe-by-default CI closes one of the most exploited supply-chain holes, and the PSF has published a playbook to follow.
− The downsideLegitimate fork-labelling, benchmark and "/test" bot workflows will break on Dec 2 unless they are rewritten.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Search your repos for pull_request_target and issue_comment this week.
  2. Move to pull_request plus workflow_run with artifacts for anything that needs privileges.
  3. Use trusted publishing (OIDC) instead of long-lived PyPI or npm tokens.
  4. Agent skills and MCP servers are distributed as repos, so they are part of your supply chain.
  5. Never pass comment text straight into a shell step; that is how prompt-style injection reaches CI.

7 · Voice AI CornerOpenAI's voice tiers · Bolna Syl

OpenAI Korea · Enterprise voice · Nov 23

OpenAI sets out its three tiers of voice AI, from chained pipelines to full-duplex GPT-Live, using a construction-site deployment as the showcase

At a Seoul briefing on Monday called "Enterprise use of Voice AI," OpenAI Korea explained how it positions its voice stack. Chained pipelines (speech-to-text, then the LLM, then text-to-speech) are for cases where exact scripted wording matters. GPT-Realtime, a single speech-to-speech model, suits counselling-style calls that need fast replies and information lookup. GPT-Live is full-duplex: it listens and speaks at the same time. It uses two layers. A front-end dialogue model keeps the conversation flowing while a separate back-end model handles complex reasoning and tool calls in parallel, so callers can change their request mid-sentence. The voice session and the back-end model are billed separately, and Korean startups called the API pricing a "considerable" burden. OpenAI also called voice the main interface for its upcoming consumer hardware.

The showcase customer was hiWorker by Hilokal. A Korean site manager speaks; GPT-4o Transcribe and the Realtime API turn that into text and speech in each worker's language, one to many, on sites with workers from 40 to 60 countries. Workers join by QR code with no app or account. A glossary built from about 1,000 industrial sites covers jargon, and the system ignores welding noise and announcements and waits for real speech. It came out of a year-long pilot with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Samsung C&T and is live at GS E&C and Daehan Construction.

＋ The upsideClear guidance from OpenAI on which tier fits which job, and a real deployment in a noisy, multilingual industrial setting.
− The downsideNo new prices or benchmarks. GPT-Live's two meters make costs hard to predict, and local startups are already pushing back on cost.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Pick the tier by use case: chained for compliance scripts, Realtime for support, Live for open-ended conversation.
  2. GPT-Live's front/back split is a pattern you can copy with any fast model plus a reasoning model.
  3. Budget for two meters on GPT-Live and model your cost per minute before choosing it.
  4. Joining by QR code with no app is a powerful onboarding pattern for frontline voice tools.
  5. Domain glossaries and handling of non-speech noise are where vertical voice products set themselves apart.
Bolna AI · Speech recognition · Nov 23

Bolna's Syl targets the one-word replies ("haan", "ji") that voice agents keep missing on Indian phone lines

Bengaluru-based Bolna (YC F25, $6.3M seed led by General Catalyst) has launched Syl, a speech-recognition model fine-tuned from an open-source base on its own call data. It is aimed at short replies and filler sounds on noisy phone lines. Bolna says more than 4M of its monthly calls contain short replies, and about 1 in 10 of them used to come back as an empty transcript. Syl claims 93% accuracy on one-word yes/no replies in held-out production calls, a 250 ms median latency, and costs "10× cheaper" than leading alternatives (no price given). It also claims a 31% drop in agents asking callers to repeat themselves and a 47% drop in callers repeating themselves. It launches in English and Hindi, with Tamil next. Bolna also previewed cross-call memory (name, language, preferred call time and progress), noting that 48% of its calls are with repeat contacts.

＋ The upsideIt fixes a specific, costly failure: misheard "yes" answers derail entire calls.
− The downsideThe numbers are self-reported, the scope is narrow (short replies, two languages), and there is no public price.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Measure how often you get empty transcripts on short replies; it is a hidden source of churn.
  2. Routing to a specialist speech-recognition model for short-answer turns is cheap and effective.
  3. Cross-call memory can cut repeat-call handling time when nearly half of calls are with returning contacts.
  4. Fine-tuning on your own production audio beats general-purpose models on your domain.
  5. Test it with code-mixed Hinglish before relying on it.
