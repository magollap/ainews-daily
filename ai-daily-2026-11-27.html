Top Story · Enterprise · Jobs · Nov 26

SAP is weighing at least 10,000 job cuts to rebuild itself around AI, its second cut of that size in two years

Bloomberg reported on Thursday afternoon, European time, that SAP is drawing up a plan to remove at least 10,000 positions. That is about 9% of a workforce of more than 110,000, and the stated goal is to reshape Europe's largest software company for AI. People familiar with the plan said every department was asked to find savings, with product, engineering and sales hit hardest. The final number could still change, because SAP has to negotiate with works councils in Germany and other European countries. SAP did not deny the report. It said AI is changing its business quickly, that it does not comment on speculation, and that no decision on "further measures" has been made. CNBC confirmed the story on Friday morning. CNBC also noted that SAP had separately disclosed a cybersecurity incident, saying it had found no evidence that customer business data was accessed.

This would be SAP's second round of roughly this size. In 2024 it restructured about 10,000 roles, mostly through voluntary exits and retraining, and total headcount barely moved because it kept hiring into AI and cloud. The 2026 plan comes after a year of buying data and AI companies: Reltio for master data, Dremio for the lakehouse, Prior Labs for tabular foundation models and TechWolf for skills intelligence. SAP also invested in Parloa (voice agents), n8n (workflow automation) and Conduct. Bloomberg places the move alongside recent cuts at Oracle and Microsoft. CNBC cites Challenger, Gray & Christmas: 121,401 US job-cut announcements in 2026 through October named AI as the reason, about a fifth of all announced cuts. Both are framed as "AI-attributed" cuts, a label companies sometimes use for ordinary cost-cutting.

Why it matters for builders
  1. Enterprise buyers will be told agents replace headcount. Have measurable outcomes ready, such as tickets resolved and minutes saved, rather than seat counts.
  2. SAP's stakes in Parloa and n8n suggest that voice and workflow agents on top of ERP are a strategic priority for SAP. Expect native competition, and partnership openings.
  3. A team in reorganisation may be slower to ship APIs and SAP certifications. Build buffers into SAP integration timelines for 2027.
  4. Clean, governed master data (Reltio) is the real moat for agents that act on business records. Design your agents to read from a source of truth.
  5. Workforce-impact questions will come up in sales and procurement reviews, so prepare a clear position on augmentation versus replacement.

2 · New AI ToolsHush · launch-day roundup

Show HN · Computer use · Open source (MIT) · Nov 26

Hush lets agents work desktop apps in a hidden display, so they never touch your cursor, focus or clipboard

Hush, an MIT-licensed project posted to Show HN on Thanksgiving, addresses the most annoying part of computer-use agents: they take over your screen. Hush starts a hidden virtual display, which it calls the "lane", and moves the windows an agent is working with onto it. Keystrokes and clicks go only to the target app, so you can keep typing in another window while the agent fills in a form. A limits file declares which apps the agent may see, read or act on, which gives a simple allow-list for desktop automation.

The agent gets seven tools: apps, observe, act, timeline, script (AppleScript/JXA), see (screenshots) and record (video with a drawn agent cursor). Hush ships as a CLI, an MCP server, or a skill for Claude Code and Codex. Its most useful feature for teams may be testing: hush save turns a successful agent run into a plain-text desktop test that replays with no model in the loop. Exploration with an LLM stays expensive, but regressions become cheap to check.

The author's benchmarks are striking but self-reported. A read-click-type-read round takes 178 ms. The tool definitions cost 972 tokens, against 2,654 to 50,397 for comparable tools. Hush scored 15/15 on Cua Driver Bench v2 background tasks with both Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, and ran 500 calls across 10 parallel sessions in 2.2 s with no errors. macOS 15+ gets every feature; Linux uses AT-SPI with a hidden X server; Windows support is "upcoming". Adoption is very early, at about 26 stars.

＋ The upsideBackground control without stealing focus is what makes desktop agents usable on a machine someone is also working on. Turning runs into replayable, model-free tests is a practical way to cut cost, and the small tool schema leaves context for the task.
− The downsideThis is a one-person project with a handful of stars. Every benchmark is the author's, and Windows, where most enterprise desktop work happens, isn't supported. Giving an agent hidden control of real apps also needs careful limits and audit logging.
Why it matters for builders
  1. If your agent automates legacy desktop software such as EHRs or dispatch consoles, background lanes remove the "don't touch the mouse" problem.
  2. Tool-schema size is a cost: about 1k tokens against 50k changes per-turn pricing and latency for long sessions.
  3. Model-free replay tests are a pattern worth copying: record once with an LLM, then regression-test cheaply in CI.
  4. Voice agents that hand off to "do it on the computer" need sub-second action rounds to keep the caller waiting less.
  5. Use the see/read/act allow-list idea in any agent permission model you design.

4 · Big AI NewsChina compute gap · LIAN IPO · small rounds

Policy · Export controls · China · Nov 26–27 · secondary report

About 45% of China's AI compute came through workarounds that US rules have now closed, and Chinese labs say their next models will be late

A Wall Street Journal report dated Nov 26, which we could only read through a detailed Seoul Economic Daily write-up, puts numbers on the effect of the remote-compute rule this paper covered earlier in the week. The figures come from an October estimate by the Institute for Progress (IFP). About 24% of Chinese AI firms' compute came from renting GPUs in overseas data centers, which the Nov 19 rule now requires a licence for. Another 22% came from smuggled Nvidia chips, which tighter enforcement is closing. Together, roughly 45% of China's effective AI compute depended on routes that are now shut or shrinking.

Chinese executives are saying so openly. Tencent president Martin Lau told investors on Nov 18 that "every company in China will be affected" and that next-generation models will take longer for now. Alibaba CEO Eddie Wu says its T-Head chips will be able to train models without outside hardware by Q1 2027. MiniMax's CEO reportedly told staff on Nov 20 to speed up adoption of Huawei and Alibaba silicon. The pressure is also hitting financing. One bank pulled out of a loan for Digital Halo's Malaysian facility, which Alibaba uses, and the $6.6B Bridge Data Centres loan was already on hold. The report adds that US labs say they now block more than 99% of attempts to distill their models, closing the other shortcut Chinese labs used.

＋ The upsideFor US policy, this is evidence that the rule works: the gap is widening and labs are announcing delays. Western model providers may keep their lead longer, and grey-market GPU capacity could return to legitimate buyers.
− The downsideThese are estimates passed through two layers of reporting, and Chinese firms are adapting quickly with domestic chips. A forced switch to Huawei and Alibaba hardware could build a fully separate stack that US vendors never sell into again.
Why it matters for builders
  1. If you depend on cheap Chinese open-weight models (DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi), plan for slower release cycles in 2027.
  2. Distillation defenses are tightening. Using frontier-model outputs to train your own models is increasingly blocked and contractually risky.
  3. Inference providers serving Chinese models may change hosting quietly. See the Ark-wrapper story in Section 5.
  4. APAC data-center financing is tightening, which may affect regional GPU availability and pricing.
  5. Keep a diverse model portfolio so one geopolitical shock doesn't take out your voice-agent backend.

GitHub · Agent infrastructure · Apache 2.0+ · trending Nov 26–27

treg wants to be the OpenRouter of agent tools: one base URL and one token in front of thousands of paid APIs, with credentials that never reach the model

treg, from the Superdesign team, gained about 500 stars in a day. It gives an agent one base URL and one token for a curated catalog of thousands of third-party endpoints, including SEO data, enrichment, scraping, and image and video generation, each billed per call. Teams can also register their own private tools: API keys, OAuth connections, CLIs and SKILL.md recipes. Everyone on the team can then use them without the secrets ever being pasted into a prompt.

Technically it is a credential-injecting proxy. Secrets are stored server-side, encrypted with Fernet and optionally AWS KMS. When the agent calls a tool, treg adds the right credential to the upstream request, so the model only ever sees the treg token. The stack is FastAPI and SQLModel, with SQLite for development and Postgres in production. It ships a Claude Code plugin, a Claude.ai connector and a treg CLI. The licence is Apache 2.0 plus extra terms that forbid running it as a competing hosted registry, so it is source-available rather than fully open source.

＋ The upsideKeeping secrets out of prompts and agent memory is a real security improvement. Per-call billing across thousands of APIs also removes dozens of separate signups.
− The downsideThe proxy becomes a single point of failure and a valuable target, since it holds every key a team owns. The custom licence clause and a hosted catalog also create lock-in to one vendor's middleman.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Adopt credential injection now, whether or not you use treg: an agent should never see raw API keys.
  2. Tool marketplaces (treg, Maglev, firecrawl's 550+ providers) are forming a new layer. Decide whether you buy it or build it.
  3. Voice agents that call CRMs mid-call can use a proxy like this to keep secrets out of transcripts and logs.
  4. Check the licence before embedding source-available infrastructure in a commercial product.
  5. Per-call pricing across tools makes cost-per-conversation easier to track. Instrument it.
Hacker News · Local inference · AMD · Nov 26

An AMD-only patch set for llama.cpp nearly doubles prefill speed, and its author warns about a wave of "vibe-coded" inference engines

A post recommending llama-cpp-rdna-boosts reached 379 points on Hacker News. It is a small set of RDNA-specific patches, re-applied to pinned llama.cpp commits and shipped as prebuilt ROCm podman images. On an AMD Radeon R9700 connected over eGPU, running a 27B Qwen-family model at Q6_K_L with multi-token prediction, prefill rose from about 700 to 1,300 tok/s and generation from about 35 to 55 tok/s.

The more useful part is the warning. November saw a wave of bespoke engines, mostly written by AI (Strata, Basalt and RustingStrata are named), that post impressive numbers partly by changing model behaviour. One example: Strata prefers mixture-of-experts experts that are already in VRAM over the experts the router actually picked. That is faster, but it is no longer the same model. The author argues that a speedup only counts if outputs are unchanged.

＋ The upsideCheaper AMD cards are becoming a credible way to self-host 27B-class models at speeds fine for interactive use.

Why it matters for builders
  1. Prefill speed sets time to first token for long voice-agent prompts, and doubling it matters more than decode speed.
  2. Any engine swap needs an output-equivalence test (logit or eval diffs), not just a tokens-per-second check.
  3. AMD is now a credible way to cut costs on on-prem or edge inference.
  4. Pin engine versions in production. Fast-moving forks break silently.
  5. MTP (multi-token prediction) is becoming standard. Check that your serving stack supports it.
Hacker News · Inference providers · Nov 26 (post dated Nov 25)

A researcher alleges that Novita, StreamLake and Ant Digital quietly route DeepSeek and GLM traffic through ByteDance's Ark

A blog post that drew 108 points on HN claims several "independent" inference providers are actually reselling ByteDance's Volcengine/BytePlus Ark for DeepSeek V4.x and GLM 5.x. It names Novita, StreamLake and Ant Digital, with SiliconFlow passing traffic through StreamLake. The author gives three pieces of evidence. Image URLs in requests were fetched from Volcano Engine IP addresses (AS137718). Tool-call IDs came back in ByteDance's format (call_00_ plus 24 characters). And ByteDance's standard censorship refusal appeared in responses. Pricing fits the theory: Ant Digital launched at BytePlus's off-peak rate of $0.12/$0.48 per million tokens and later dropped below it to $0.09/$0.36.

The providers had not responded at the time of writing, and the post is one person's investigation. If it holds up, customers who chose these providers for a zero-data-retention promise may have had prompts processed by a party they never agreed to.

＋ The upsideSimple fingerprinting (ID formats, egress IPs, refusal text) lets any customer audit where their traffic really goes.
− The downsideIf true, privacy and data-retention promises are only as good as an undisclosed upstream provider, and cheap prices may simply be resold capacity.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Ask inference vendors in writing who processes your tokens and where.
  2. Run your own canary checks: send a known image URL and watch which IPs fetch it.
  3. Call recordings and PHI should go only to providers with contractual sub-processor lists.
  4. Unusually cheap rates for open models should prompt a check of their supply chain.
  5. Keep provider-agnostic routing so you can drop a vendor overnight.

6 · DevOps & KubernetesKueue patches · quiet elsewhere

kubernetes-sigs/kueue · v0.20.8 & v0.19.15 · Nov 26

Kueue patches add a fair-sharing preemption fallback and fix stuck DRA GPU jobs, but renamed log keys will break dashboards

Kueue, the Kubernetes batch-queueing system many teams use to share GPUs between training and inference jobs, shipped v0.20.8 and v0.19.15 on Thursday. The change most likely to cause trouble is in operations, not features. Logging was brought in line with a project-wide policy: per-object messages moved from verbosity 2 to 3, and many structured keys were renamed (for example replica-role became replicaRole). Any log-based alert, Loki query or dashboard that matches the old keys will silently stop matching after the upgrade.

There is a new alpha feature gate, FairSharingPreemptWithinClusterQueueFallback. It is off by default and requires FairSharingVerifyFinalTargets. When fair-sharing preemption across queues finds no valid victims, Kueue can now fall back to preempting inside the same ClusterQueue instead of leaving the workload pending. For AI clusters, the DRA (Dynamic Resource Allocation) fixes matter most. CEL selectors on device.allowMultipleAllocations now match correctly, workloads with several CEL-selected device requests no longer get stuck pending, and AdminAccess requests in allocationMode: All no longer reserve quota when no device matches. Other fixes: Admission Fair Sharing now picks its victim from the most-used LocalQueue, elastic-job quota leaks are fixed, MultiKueue accepts JAXJob on worker clusters, a TAS panic during multi-node failure eviction is gone, and several 403s under OwnerReferencesPermissionEnforcement are fixed. No security fixes were listed.

＋ The upsideGPU jobs that use DRA and CEL device selection now schedule reliably, and the fallback reduces workloads stuck pending forever in busy shared clusters.
− The downsideRenaming log keys in a patch release is a surprise for operators, and observability will quietly break unless you update queries before upgrading.
Why it matters for builders
  1. Before upgrading, search your alerting for old Kueue log keys and update them.
  2. If you schedule inference GPUs with DRA, this patch fixes real stuck-pod bugs, so upgrade.
  3. Try the fallback gate in staging if training jobs regularly starve your real-time inference queue.
  4. JAX shops running multi-cluster can now use MultiKueue with JAXJob.
  5. Keep realtime voice-model serving in a separate ClusterQueue with guaranteed quota.
