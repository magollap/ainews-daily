Claude Mythos ported Claude to AMD GPUs in three days

The Decoder · Nov 11, 14:25 UTC · statements by co-founder Tom Brown

Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown said the company now trains and serves Claude on four major chip families and schedules them as one interchangeable compute pool. The headline claim: Anthropic's restricted frontier model, Claude Mythos, did most of the work of porting Claude to AMD accelerators in about three days with very little human help. Brown said that kind of port normally takes six to twelve months.

Claimed: 4 chip families in one pool · AMD port in ~3 days vs 6–12 months typical · "compute multipliers" to get more out of each chip · a chip-leasing program to lower total cost of ownership. Not disclosed: which kernels were rewritten, how performance compares across chips, or whether the AMD fleet serves production traffic.
PositiveIf it holds up, AI-written kernels weaken CUDA lock-in. More capacity and pricing leverage for Anthropic should mean steadier rate limits and lower prices for Claude customers.
NegativeIt's a single-source claim with no published benchmarks. A port that "runs" isn't one that is tuned, and mixed fleets can cause small differences in output between chip types.
Why it matters
  1. More silicon behind Claude means more headroom for high-concurrency voice and agent traffic.
  2. Plan for small numeric differences across hardware: pin seeds and evals, not exact outputs.
  3. Agent-written infrastructure code is now a real lever. Consider it for your own porting chores.
  4. This lands the same day Nvidia's leadership is in doubt, so expect "multi-silicon" in every vendor pitch.
  5. Ask providers for per-region, per-hardware latency SLOs, not averages.

3 · LLM & Model Updates new models · pricing · benchmarks

Google's Gemini Decision 1: a model that only decides

Google Keyword blog, @googledevs, MarkTechPost, InfoWorld · Nov 11

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API, powered by a new model, gemini-decision-1. It doesn't generate text. It reads a shared input once and returns calibrated answers to a set of closed questions: a probability for yes/no, a pick from a list, or a score on a scale you define. It is built for routing, tool selection, safety checks and grading inside apps and agents, where waiting for and paying for generated tokens is wasted.

Inputs: text, image, audio, video, PDF; up to 256K tokens of shared context · Batch: up to 1,000 independent questions per request · Latency: 150–250 ms, one prefill pass · Price: $0.10 per 1M input tokens, $0 output, free tier in AI Studio · Claim: ~5× cheaper than Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite on Google's shared benchmark suite · SDKs: google-genai, OpenRouter, LangChain, Vercel AI SDK, LiteLLM, Pydantic AI, Pipecat, LiveKit, OpenCode · Vertex support "coming soon".

Google already uses it internally: Gemini Notebook picks a model and thinking level per query with it, and Search uses it to flag misuse. It joins a new category of "decision models": TypeSafe's Jev (September) and OpenAI's Decisions API (October) came first. Independent reviewers note Jev is still cheaper.

PositiveFree output tokens and sub-250 ms latency make it practical to check every conversational turn. Native Pipecat and LiveKit support means it plugs into voice stacks on day one.
NegativeThe benchmarks are Google's own. Calibration on your domain still needs checking. Vertex/enterprise support isn't here yet, and it's another vendor dependency.
Why it matters
  1. Swap slow "LLM-as-router" calls for a 200 ms decision on every voice turn: intent, escalation, end-of-call.
  2. Run dozens of policy and safety checks per turn in one request at near-zero marginal cost.
  3. Use the confidence scores to send uncertain cases to a frontier model or a human.
  4. Grade agent transcripts at scale for QA and RL reward signals.
  5. Benchmark it against OpenAI's Decisions API and Jev on your own labeled data before standardizing.

CNCF builds the fence around AI agents on Kubernetes

heise iX (Nov 12), VMblog Day-1 recap, CNCF announcements (Nov 11)

This KubeCon's theme was running agents safely. CNCF accepted several projects aimed at agent isolation and efficiency. Nvidia's OpenShell enforces policy on what agents may do. Google's gVisor provides a user-space kernel sandbox for untrusted code. Both join Agent Substrate, Google's actor-style layer that suspends idle agent sessions and resumes them on the next message so they don't hold CPUs or GPUs. Broadcom contributed a VM API so virtual machines can be managed as Kubernetes objects, next to the newly graduated KubeVirt. CNCF now hosts 231 projects (32 new) and counts 21.2M cloud-native developers.

PositiveA vendor-neutral, open stack for isolating agents and using GPUs efficiently, with policy and sandboxing as first-class features.
NegativeMany young projects stacked together means more complexity to operate. gVisor adds syscall overhead, and governance is still settling.
Why it matters
  1. Suspend-on-idle fits voice agents well: long sessions with little compute in between.
  2. Sandbox every agent that runs tools or code. gVisor is now a CNCF default.
  3. Put agent action policy in the platform, not just in prompts.
  4. Platform teams can offer "agents as a workload" on the clusters they already run.
  5. Track Agent Substrate and kagent 1.0 (GA Nov 10) for production readiness.

Decagon's Alto runs conversation and reasoning in parallel

Decagon blog (via Unite.AI) · Nov 11

Alto is a Decagon Labs model that controls the conversational layer of a full-duplex voice agent: when to speak, when to hold back, and what to say while a separate reasoning model works through the actual task. Decagon reports average response times around 700 ms and higher resolution rates on live calls. It trained Alto on 90M+ simulated calls weighted toward ambiguous moments. Alto works alongside its Voice 3 and Chord models.

PositiveSeparating "talk" from "think" removes dead air on hard turns without dumbing down the reasoning.
NegativeThe figures are vendor-reported, and filler speech can make promises the reasoning model then overturns.
Why it matters
  1. A dual-model (fast talker, slow thinker) design is becoming the standard.
  2. Simulated-call training at scale is a moat. Build your own simulator.
  3. ~700 ms is the bar to beat for natural turn latency.
  4. Guard against the conversational model committing to actions too early.
  5. Measure resolution rate, not just latency.

DeepL's Aure keeps tone and emotion in live speech translation

Trending Topics, The Decoder · Nov 11

Aure continuously analyzes incoming speech and carries the speaker's tone, emotion, emphasis and pace, including sarcasm and mood shifts, into the translated voice. The previous system only captured those traits at the start. It launches in eight languages: EN, DE, ES, FR, IT, JA, PT and KO. No benchmarks have been published yet.

PositiveCross-lingual calls that sound human, useful for multilingual support desks.
NegativeNo public evals, a limited language list, and emotional mimicry raises consent questions.
Why it matters
  1. Live multilingual voice agents become realistic without separate per-language bots.
  2. Prosody transfer affects customer satisfaction as much as word accuracy.
  3. Check latency added per hop before putting it in a call path.
  4. Disclose AI translation to callers.
  5. Compare with speech-to-speech models from ElevenLabs and Google.

Quick takes

Gemini Live on CarPlay (Nov 11): rolling out worldwide to iOS users in every Gemini Live language. It's voice-only with no on-screen text, you can interrupt it, and it can summarize Gmail and check Calendar. You must have used Gemini Live on the phone first. The car is now a real channel for voice agents.

The dark side (NPR, Nov 11): swatters are using AI-cloned voices to fool 911 dispatchers. Expect pressure for voice provenance, watermarking and caller-verification rules that will affect legitimate outbound agents too.

Beeble SwitchX 3.0 relights scenes but keeps the actor

Broadcast Now · Nov 11

Beeble's video-to-video model regenerates everything around a performer, guided by reference images, floor plans or storyboards. Its "Source-Locked Rendering" keeps the original performance inside the actor's alpha matte frame by frame.

NegativeA niche, pro-workflow tool. Matte quality limits the results.
